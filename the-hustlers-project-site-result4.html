Shared Purpose

Goal

Our shared goal for the project is to improve the business, make operations more efficient, and learn and improve technical skills with hands-on experience.

What Success Looks Like

Success for the team means that the team successfully designed an efficient team for users.

Communication

Platform

Response Time

Less than 24 hours on both weekdays and weekends.

Meeting Cadence

Preferably Sunday afternoons (after 4 PM), biweekly, or once a week when necessary.

Decision-Making

How Decisions Are Made

We will try to communicate and have everyone aligned before making a significant decision. We can also take a majority vote and come to a consensus.

How Disagreements Are Handled

The process will be communicating directly with the individual where the disagreement arises first.

Working Agreements

Participation

Everyone showing up to meetings, contributing to discussions, sharing their thoughts, and completing the tasks on time.

Accountability

We will do check-ins and meetings ahead of time (before a deadline) to ensure everyone is on track.

Deadlines

We will set internal deadlines on when the team would like to complete tasks. The timing depends on the task, but it would typically be one week beforehand.

Conflict Resolution

Unresponsive Teammate

The team will first send a direct message, but after the dedicated response time, we will send emails to their official school account. After that, we may contact the instructor if the teammate is unresponsive for longer periods of time.

How Concerns Are Raised

Team members can raise their concerns in meetings and class sessions, or notify others through text messages, our primary communication channel.

When to Involve the Instructor

When team members have unprofessional disagreements, or when a discussed issue continues to persist.

Success Metrics

What Will Make Us Proud

Seeing the product work and having a simple service that can be translated across businesses.

Behaviors That Define Success

Holding each other accountable and making sure we all communicate.

Chapter Three

Client Interview Outline

What We Know, and What We Still Need to Learn

From our research, we know the business does not have a digital booking system or website. We need to confirm what their current booking methods are, apart from social media. We also need to confirm their strengths, weaknesses, opportunities for growth, and current challenges.

Background & Context Questions
  1. Can you share what inspired you to start the business?
  2. Do you eventually want to scale this business, or keep it at the same level it currently is?
  3. What are some characteristics of your business's clientele?
Process Questions
  1. Can you walk us through the current booking process?
  2. How do clients confirm their booking? Do they pay a deposit?
  3. Where do you usually notice the booking process slowing down?
  4. Have you previously tried a solution to make your booking system more efficient?
Pain Point & User Experience Questions
  1. Are there times when you've accidentally double-booked clients? Can you tell us more about that experience?
  2. Tell us more about times when there was miscommunication, or when a client expressed dissatisfaction with the service. How did you find a resolution?
  3. Within the process of booking a client, what are the most frustrating challenges you face?
Communication & Success Questions
  1. Do you save clients' contact information based on how often they book with you?
  2. How would you prefer to save your clients' information, separate from their personal data?
Constraints Questions

We don't know exactly what the end result would look like, or whether our client would like it — so we need to discuss the client's expectations. What are they?

  1. Are there any privacy concerns regarding personal customer information?
  2. With your limits on transportation, what compromises do you make with clients to make sure they still book with you?
